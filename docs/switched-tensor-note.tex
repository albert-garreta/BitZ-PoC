\title{Switched-Tensor Lookup Certification for Wide-Word\\
Integer Evaluation over Binary Fields\\[0.6ex]
\large A working note}
\author{F2Z project notes}
\date{2026-07-20 \quad (v0.1 --- design note; costs are modeled, not measured)}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We consider polynomial commitment schemes for $W$-bit integer data committed
bit-wise over a characteristic-two field, where an evaluation claim
$y=\MLE[\INT(D)](r)\bmod q$ is reduced to weighted integer folds carried in
the exponent of $\K=\F_{2^{128}}$ and certified by grand-product GKR
(``forest'') arguments. The deployed certification costs one bit-affine leaf
per committed bit and is therefore blind to the word width $W$: at equal
total bit count, wide-word instances cannot outperform $W=1$ instances. We
describe a \emph{switched tensor order} for the per-leaf weights
$w_b\cdot 2^j$: the narrow place-value factors $2^j$ are moved to the outer
(aggregation) axis, where they are handled in the clear, and the wide
$\sim\!100$-bit evaluation weights $w_b$ are moved into \emph{public}
lookup tables shared by all $W$ bit-planes. The sharing shrinks the table
side of the lookup argument by a factor of exactly $W$, which is the term
that made previous lookup-based designs uncompetitive. Queries are bound by
a characteristic-two-sound certificate; we give two instantiations, a
multiplicative grand-product multiset argument built entirely from the
deployed forest machinery, and a multiplicity-free logarithmic-derivative
(pushforward) argument. We prove soundness of the multiset instantiation,
give a cost model placing the prover at $\approx 0.8$--$1.05\times$ the
per-bit baseline (i.e.\ a modeled speedup of $\approx 0.95$--$1.3\times$,
against a required decision bar of $1.5\times$), and specify the
measurement gate that must adjudicate the design. A by-product of the
construction is that weight chunking disappears entirely: the wide weights
never meet the exponent-capacity bound.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Setting.}
A prover commits to a bit tensor
$D\in\{0,1\}^{2^t\times W\times 2^s}$, $W=2^w$, interpreted as a
$2^t\times 2^s$ matrix of $W$-bit integers
$\INT(D)(b,c)=\sum_{j<W}2^j\,D(b,j,c)$, for a total of
$N=2^{t+w+s}$ committed bits. The commitment is a linear-map-friendly PCS
over $\F_2$ (in the reference implementation: a ring-switched, recursively
composed proximity-tested code) whose openings discharge multilinear
evaluation claims about the committed bits over $\K=\F_{2^{128}}$. The
statement is a wide-modulus evaluation
\[
y \;=\; \sum_{b,c} \eq(b,r_1)\,\eq(c,r_2)\cdot \INT(D)(b,c) \pmod q,
\qquad q = 2^{100}-15 .
\]
Lifting the row weights to canonical representatives
$w_b := \eq(b,r_1)\bmod q\in[0,q)$ and keeping the column weights
$w'_c:=\eq(c,r_2)\in\F_q$ at full width, the claim factors through the
per-column integer folds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fold}
u_c \;=\; \sum_b w_b\cdot \INT(D)(b,c) \;\in\;\Z_{\ge 0},
\qquad
y \;=\; \sum_c w'_c\, u_c \pmod q .
\end{equation}
Characteristic two prohibits certifying \eqref{eq:fold} by a sumcheck over
$\K$ (integers lifted into $\K$ collapse to parity), so the fold rides
\emph{multiplicatively}: for a generator $\alpha$ of $\K^\times$
(of order $\ord(\alpha)=2^{128}-1$), the map $n\mapsto\alpha^n$ is
injective on $[0,2^{128}-1)$, and the prover certifies $\alpha^{u_c}$ as a
grand product of \emph{bit-affine leaves}
\[
\ell(b,j) \;=\; 1 + D(b,j,c)\cdot\bigl(\alpha^{w_b 2^j}-1\bigr)
\;=\; \bigl(\alpha^{w_b 2^j}\bigr)^{D(b,j,c)},
\]
one per committed bit, by a lazy merged GKR product forest. The bases
$\alpha^{w_b 2^j}$ are \emph{public}; the leaf-layer claim is linear in the
committed bits and discharges through the PCS opening at no extra
certification cost. Because $u_c$ may exceed the exponent capacity, the
weights are chunked into $c_w$-bit limbs with $c_w = 127-t-W$ and the
protocol repeats per limb; this detail is orthogonal here and, as we will
see, vanishes in the present construction.

\paragraph{The question.}
The prover cost of the baseline is $\Theta(N)$ multiplications in $\K$
--- one bit-affine leaf, with its share of sumcheck rounds, per committed
bit --- with a measured constant of $c_{\mathrm{bit}}\approx 10.5$\,ns per
bit on the reference hardware. This cost is blind to $W$. Wide words carry
visible structure (all $W$ bits of a cell share one row weight; within a
cell, the bases form a Frobenius orbit $\alpha^{w_b2^j}=\varphi^j(\alpha^{w_b})$),
and the question is whether any of it can push the prover \emph{below}
one leaf per bit. Prior attempts in this project --- an octet-grouped
lookup at $W=1$, a wide-outer tensor decomposition, base-$2^k$
weight slicing --- were all measured or adjudicated at
$\le 1.0\times$; a recurring accounting, which we record as
Remark~\ref{rem:conservation}, explains why: certified work is paid per
\emph{private} exponent bit, and only public-base precomputation absorbs
weight width for free.

\paragraph{Contribution.}
We observe that the per-leaf weight $w_b\cdot 2^j$ is a tensor with a
narrow factor ($2^j$, one nonzero bit) and a wide factor
($w_b$, $\sim\!100$ bits), and that all prior designs placed the wide
factor on the private (certified) axis. Switching the tensor order places:
\begin{itemize}
\item the \emph{wide} factors inside public lookup tables
  $T_\beta[a]=\alpha^{\ip{a}{w_\beta}}$ indexed by octets of committed
  bits --- the width-free position --- and, decisively, \emph{shared by
  all $W$ bit-planes and all columns}, shrinking the lookup argument's
  table side by a factor of $W$;
\item the \emph{narrow} factors $2^j$ on the outer aggregation axis, where
  they are handled in the clear (or by a companion commitment), never
  inside a certified exponent.
\end{itemize}
Sections~\ref{sec:construction}--\ref{sec:soundness} give the construction
and its soundness; Section~\ref{sec:costs} prices it honestly against the
measured baseline and specifies the measurement gate. We emphasize
up front that this note argues \emph{soundness}, not \emph{profit}: the
cost model straddles the project's $1.5\times$ adoption bar, and four
predecessor designs died at measurement.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

\paragraph{Notation.}
$\K=\F_{2^{128}}$ with Frobenius $\varphi(x)=x^2$;
$\varphi$ is a field automorphism fixing $\F_2$, of order $128$, so
$\varphi^{-k}=\varphi^{128-k}$ is locally computable. $B=\{0,1\}$. For
$x\in B^n$, $\eq(x,r)=\prod_i (x_ir_i+(1-x_i)(1-r_i))$. For
$f:B^n\to\K$, $\mhat f$ denotes its multilinear extension and
$\mhat f(r)=\sum_x \eq(x,r)f(x)$. $\alpha$ is a fixed
verifier-checked generator of $\K^\times$. All committed vectors are
$\F_2$-valued; the PCS discharges claims of the form
$\sum_x \lambda(x)\,M(x)$ for public $\K$-valued $\lambda$ and committed
bits $M$ (``linear read-offs''), and batched multilinear evaluations of
$M$ at transcript-derived points.

We use the merged product forest as a black box: given per-tree leaf
vectors over $\K$, it certifies all tree roots with prover cost
proportional to the number of leaves, at measured per-leaf constants
$c_{\mathrm{bit}}$ (bit-affine leaves, public bases, free linear
discharge) and $c_{\mathrm{dense}}\approx 2c_{\mathrm{bit}}$ (dense
prover-supplied leaf values), plus a leaf-layer evaluation claim that the
caller must discharge.

\begin{lemma}[Twisted evaluation]\label{lem:twist}
Let $f:B^n\to\K$ and let $f^{(2)}(x)=f(x)^2$ pointwise. Then for every
$r\in\K^n$,
\[
\mhat{f^{(2)}}(r) \;=\; \Bigl(\, \mhat f\bigl(\varphi^{-1}(r)\bigr)
\Bigr)^2 ,
\]
where $\varphi^{-1}$ is applied coordinate-wise. Iterating,
$\mhat{f^{(2^k)}}(r)=\varphi^k\!\bigl(\mhat f(\varphi^{-k}(r))\bigr)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Squaring is additive in characteristic two, so
$\mhat f(s)^2=\sum_x \eq(x,s)^2 f(x)^2$. For $x_i\in\{0,1\}$ each factor
of $\eq$ squares to
$(x_is_i+(1-x_i)(1-s_i))^2 = x_is_i^2+(1-x_i)(1-s_i)^2
= x_is_i^2+(1-x_i)(1-s_i^2)$,
using $x_i^2=x_i$ and $(1-s)^2=1-s^2$ in characteristic two. Hence
$\eq(x,s)^2=\eq(x,s^{(2)})$ with $s^{(2)}=(s_i^2)_i$, and substituting
$s=\varphi^{-1}(r)$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Width conservation, informal]\label{rem:conservation}
The statement \eqref{eq:fold} involves $q_{\mathrm{bits}}\cdot N$
weight-bit$\times$data-bit products. A certified object whose private
input is a $c$-bit committed index accounts for at most $c$ committed
bits per query, while a \emph{public} table entry may absorb arbitrarily
many weight bits at no certified cost. Consequently: private-base
exponentiation pays one certified unit per exponent bit; public-base
precomputation is width-free. The baseline leaf is the $c=1$ endpoint
(one committed bit selecting one public constant, certificate free by
linearity). The present construction is the $c=8$ point of the same
family: eight committed bits select one of $256$ public constants, and
the price of leaving the endpoint is a membership certificate. Everything
in Section~\ref{sec:costs} is an accounting of that certificate.
\end{remark}

\section{The switched tensor order}\label{sec:construction}

Fix the octet parameter $n_2=8$ (any $n_2\le 16$ works; $n_2=8$ is the
knee in our model). Assume $t\ge 3$ and partition the row axis into
octets: for $\beta\in[2^{t-3}]$ let
$w_\beta=(w_{8\beta},\dots,w_{8\beta+7})$ and, for plane $j\in[W]$ and
column $c$, let
\[
a_{j,\beta,c} \;=\;
\bigl(D(8\beta+i,\, j,\, c)\bigr)_{i<8} \in B^8
\]
be the corresponding committed octet.

\subsection{Public tables and the sharing factor}

\begin{definition}[Row-octet tables]\label{def:tables}
For each $\beta$, define the public table
$T_\beta : B^8 \to \K^\times$,
\[
T_\beta[a] \;=\; \alpha^{\ip{a}{w_\beta}}
\;=\; \prod_{i<8}\bigl(\alpha^{w_{8\beta+i}}\bigr)^{a_i}.
\]
\end{definition}
There are $|E|=2^{t-3}\cdot 2^8 = 2^{t+5}$ table entries in total, built
incrementally in one $\K$-multiplication per entry from the $2^t$ bases
$\alpha^{w_b}$ (which both parties already compute in the baseline). Two
properties are load-bearing:

\emph{(i) Sharing.} $T_\beta$ depends only on the row octet --- not on
the plane $j$ or the column $c$. All $W\cdot 2^s$ (plane, column) pairs
query the same $2^{t+5}$ entries. A $W=1$ instance of the same total size
has $2^{t+w}$ rows with pairwise distinct weights and hence $2^{t+w+5}$
entries: the table side is a factor $W$ larger. This factor is the entire
advantage of wide words, and it lands exactly on the terms (table
construction, multiplicity bookkeeping, table-side certification) that
dominated and killed the prior octet-grouped design at $W=1$.

\emph{(ii) Product form.} $T_\beta$ is multilinear-with-multiplicative-
structure in its index: for any $\rho\in\K^8$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closedform}
\sum_{a\in B^8}\eq(a,\rho)\,T_\beta[a]
\;=\; \prod_{i<8}\Bigl((1-\rho_i) + \rho_i\,\alpha^{w_{8\beta+i}}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
so the verifier evaluates the table's multilinear extension at any point
in $O(2^{t-3}\cdot 8)$ multiplications given the $\alpha^{w_b}$ --- the
table is never committed and never materialized by the verifier.

\subsection{Per-plane folds; chunking disappears}

Write the statement with the place values outermost:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:switched}
u_c \;=\; \sum_{j<W} 2^j\, v_{j,c},
\qquad
v_{j,c} \;:=\; \sum_{\beta}\ip{a_{j,\beta,c}}{w_\beta}
\;=\; \sum_b w_b\, D(b,j,c) .
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Magnitude]\label{lem:magnitude}
$0\le v_{j,c} < 2^t q < 2^{t+100}$. In particular, for $t\le 27$ every
per-plane fold is individually below $2^{127}<\ord(\alpha)$, and the map
$v\mapsto\alpha^v$ is injective on the occurring values.
\end{lemma}

The protocol therefore certifies the \emph{per-plane} folds $v_{j,c}$
multiplicatively and performs the outer aggregation
$u_c=\sum_j 2^j v_{j,c}$ and the read-off
$y=\sum_c w'_c u_c \bmod q$ entirely in $\F_q$, on the clear (or
committed, \S\ref{sec:committedfolds}) values $v_{j,c}$. Two consequences:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No weight chunking.} The wide weights live whole inside the
public table entries; no certified exponent ever multiplies a weight by a
cell value. The baseline's limb decomposition
($c_w = 127-t-W$, $L=\lceil q_{\mathrm{bits}}/c_w\rceil$ instances) is
gone for every $t\le 27$ and every $W$ --- in particular the forced
$L=2$ of the baseline at $W=32$.
\item \textbf{Payload.} There are $W\cdot 2^s$ per-plane folds instead of
$2^s$ per-column folds. Sent in the clear they cost $16\,W2^s$ bytes
(1\,MiB at $W=32$, $s=11$) and $W2^s$ verifier exponentiations; at scale
the committed-fold companion of \S\ref{sec:committedfolds} is mandatory.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[Why the plane axis is not folded in the exponent]
\label{rem:wraparound}
It is tempting to aggregate $\sum_j 2^j v_{j,c}$ inside the forest by
Frobenius-twisted combines $x\cdot\varphi^{2^k}(y)\cdot C^{\kappa}$ with
$C=\alpha^{-q}$ and committed correction digits $\kappa$, keeping
intermediate exponents reduced modulo $q$. This is \emph{unsound as
stated}: correction digits are constrained only by their committed
widths, the representable corrections span far more than $128$ bits of
freedom, and $\ord(\alpha)=2^{128}-1\not\equiv 0 \pmod q$ --- so a
malicious prover can grind $\kappa$ to wrap the exponent around
$\ord(\alpha)$ and shift the final value by a nonzero multiple of
$\ord(\alpha)\bmod q$, changing the read-off. Repairing this requires
range certificates on every intermediate reduced exponent, at which
point the design is strictly worse than keeping the plane axis in the
clear. The per-plane formulation \eqref{eq:switched} avoids the entire
issue; no Frobenius twist appears anywhere in the prover's circuit.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The value forests}

For each plane--column pair $(j,c)$ the prover runs a product tree of
depth $t-3$ over the octet values
\[
V_{j,c} \;=\; \prod_\beta v^{\mathrm{leaf}}_{j,\beta,c},
\qquad
v^{\mathrm{leaf}}_{j,\beta,c} \;\stackrel{!}{=}\; T_\beta[a_{j,\beta,c}],
\]
so that, for an honest prover, $V_{j,c}=\alpha^{v_{j,c}}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:magnitude} bounds the exponent). All $W2^s$ trees run in
one merged forest call. The leaves are \emph{prover-supplied} table reads
--- $N/8$ dense leaves in place of $N$ bit-affine leaves --- and the
entire soundness burden moves to certifying the marked equality
$\stackrel{!}{=}$, i.e.\ that every supplied leaf equals the public table
entry at the committed index. That certificate is the subject of the next
section.

\section{The certification layer}\label{sec:cert}

\subsection{Fingerprints and multiplicities}

After the commitments are fixed (see \S\ref{sec:fs} for the transcript
order), the verifier draws $\gamma_{\mathrm{pos}}\in\K^{t-3}$,
$\gamma_{\mathrm{idx}}\in\K^{8}$, $\mu,\delta\in\K$ and both parties
define the fingerprint of a (position, index, value) triple:
\[
\fp(\beta,a,v) \;=\; \ip{\gamma_{\mathrm{pos}}}{\mathrm{bits}(\beta)}
+ \ip{\gamma_{\mathrm{idx}}}{a} + \mu\, v \;\in\;\K .
\]
Let $Q=\{(j,\beta,c)\}$ be the query set, $|Q|=N/8$, with query triple
$\tau_q=(\beta_q,a_q,v_q)$, and let $E=\{(\beta,a)\}$ be the table
domain, $|E|=2^{t+5}$, with table triple $\tau_e=(\beta,a,T_\beta[a])$.
The prover counts multiplicities
\[
m_e \;=\; \#\{\,q\in Q : (\beta_q,a_q)=e\,\} \;\le\; W2^s ,
\]
and commits them in binary as $s{+}w{+}1$ extra bit-planes over $E$
($\approx (s{+}w{+}1)2^{t+5}$ bits, i.e.\ $3$--$5\%$ of $N$ at
$s\in[9,11]$). Crucially $m$ is a function of $D$ alone --- no challenge
enters it --- so the multiplicity planes are part of the \emph{same}
first-round commitment as the data.

\subsection{The multiplicative grand-product multiset argument}

The lookup relation is certified by the polynomial identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiset}
\prod_{q\in Q}\bigl(\delta-\fp(\tau_q)\bigr)
\;=\;
\prod_{e\in E}\bigl(\delta-\fp(\tau_e)\bigr)^{m_e},
\end{equation}
with both sides certified as grand products:

\paragraph{Witness side.}
The factors $\delta-\fp(\tau_q)$ are grouped by $(j,c)$ into $W2^s$ trees
of depth $t-3$ --- the same shape as the value forests --- and run as a
second claim family in the \emph{same} merged forest call. The leaf at
query $q$ is $G_{\beta}[a_q]$ where
$G_\beta[a] = \delta - \ip{\gamma_{\mathrm{pos}}}{\mathrm{bits}(\beta)}
- \ip{\gamma_{\mathrm{idx}}}{a} - \mu T_\beta[a]$
is another public per-octet $256$-entry table (built from $T_\beta$ by
$256$ additions).

\paragraph{Table side.}
Decomposing $m_e=\sum_k 2^k m_{e,k}$,
\[
\prod_e (\delta-\fp(\tau_e))^{m_e}
= \prod_{e}\prod_{k\le s+w}
\Bigl(\underbrace{\varphi^k\bigl(\delta-\fp(\tau_e)\bigr)}_{\textstyle
B_{e,k}}\Bigr)^{m_{e,k}}
= \prod_{e,k}\bigl(1 + m_{e,k}\,(B_{e,k}-1)\bigr),
\]
which is a forest of \emph{bit-affine leaves with public bases} over the
committed multiplicity bits --- exactly the baseline leaf shape, with its
free linear discharge, over $(s{+}w{+}1)\,2^{t+5}$ leaves. The verifier's
exit evaluations of the base family use the product form
\eqref{eq:closedform} together with Lemma~\ref{lem:twist}:
$\sum_e \eq(e,\rho)\,\varphi^k(\delta-\fp(\tau_e))
= \varphi^k\bigl(X(\varphi^{-k}(\rho))\bigr)$ where
$X(\sigma)=\delta-(\text{linear terms})-\mu\,\mhat T(\sigma)$ is closed
form. The table is never committed.

\paragraph{The shared byte-driven discharge.}
The two dense claim families (values $T_\beta[a_q]$ and fingerprints
$G_\beta[a_q]$) exit the merged forest at one shared point with leaf-MLE
claims that are both affine in the same two residuals: the linear
read-offs of the position/index bits (free) and the evaluation
$\mhat{v}(\rho)$ of the table-read vector. That residual is discharged by
\emph{byte-driven case-LUT rounds}: the leaf value at each query position
is one gather from a public $256$-case table keyed by the committed
octet, and eight bit-driven sumcheck rounds bind the octet bits against
progressively folded case tables ($256\to 128\to\cdots$), after which the
remaining claim is a plain multilinear evaluation of the committed bits,
batched into the PCS opening. This is the depth-$8$ extension of the
deployed depth-$2$/$3$ bit-driven leaf rounds; the project's byte-table
measurements support pricing these rounds at dense-round parity. This
discharge is what closes the ``free wires'' loophole: leaf values are
never free prover inputs; they are, verifiably, functions of the
committed bits through public tables fixed before $\delta$.

\subsection{Transcript order}\label{sec:fs}
(1) Commit $D$ together with the multiplicity planes $m$ (both
challenge-independent). (2) The claim $(r,y)$ is fixed; weights
$w_b, w'_c$ are public functions of $r$. (3) [Committed-fold variant
only: commit the fold bits, \S\ref{sec:committedfolds}.] (4) Draw
$\gamma_{\mathrm{pos}},\gamma_{\mathrm{idx}},\mu,\delta$. (5) Run the
merged forests (value + fingerprint families; multiplicity forest),
the shared discharge, and the openings. (6) Verifier: rebuild
$\alpha^{w_b}$ (as in the baseline), check the multiset root identity
\eqref{eq:multiset}, check $V$-roots against the per-plane folds
(exponentiation in the clear variant; the linkage of
\S\ref{sec:committedfolds} otherwise), range-check the folds, and
compute $y=\sum_c w'_c\sum_j 2^j v_{j,c}\bmod q$.

\subsection{Committed folds (companion gadget)}\label{sec:committedfolds}
At scale the $W2^s$ clear folds are replaced by a committed
bit-decomposition bound by a static exponentiation forest: the fold bits
(at most $t{+}100$ planes per fold; the bit width itself enforces the
range bound) are committed after step (2); tiny bit-affine trees with the
public Frobenius base chain $\alpha^{2^k}=\varphi^k(\alpha)$ certify
$\alpha^{v_{j,c}}$; a shared-challenge equality of root-MLE claims links
them to the value forests' roots without ever sending roots; and the
$\F_q$ read-off becomes a single-column instance of the mod-$q$ protocol
itself over the fold bits, terminating in one clear $128$-bit integer.
At $s\ge 10$, $W=32$, the fold-bit instance is large enough to sit inside
the audited parameter regime of the underlying opener. We omit details;
the gadget is specified in the project's u-suppression note and is
independent of the present construction.

\subsection{Instantiation B: the pushforward (logup*) certificate}
\label{sec:logup}
The fingerprint and multiplicity forests can be replaced by a
logarithmic-derivative argument in its indexed, multiplicity-free
(pushforward) form: query terms $\eq$-weighted as
$1/(\delta-\fp(\tau_q))$, table terms $Y_e/(\delta-\fp(\tau_e))$ with
$Y$ the pushforward of the query weights along the index map, certified
by a fraction-addition GKR. Characteristic-two soundness of this form is
established in the project's route-(b) certificate
(\texttt{quotient\_gkr.rs}), with the fraction layer's per-term cost
measured at $\phi\approx 1.25$--$1.5$ product-forest terms. Its
distinguishing adder is that $Y$ ($|E|$ field elements) is
prover-supplied and must be bound --- a second commitment of
$\approx 2^{7-s}N$ bits --- whereas the multiset instantiation commits
only the $3$--$5\%$ multiplicity planes and reuses the deployed forest
machinery wholesale. The model of Section~\ref{sec:costs} slightly
favors the multiset arm at $s\le 10$ and the pushforward arm at
$s\ge 11$; both should be gated in the same harness, since they share
the value forests, tables, and discharge.

\section{Soundness}\label{sec:soundness}

\begin{lemma}[Fingerprint injectivity]\label{lem:collision}
Fix any two distinct triples $\tau\ne\tau'$ occurring among
$\{\tau_q\}\cup\{\tau_e\}$. Then
$\Pr_{\gamma,\mu}[\fp(\tau)=\fp(\tau')]\le 1/|\K|$, and by a union bound
all occurring triples receive distinct fingerprints except with
probability at most $(|Q|+|E|)^2/|\K|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\fp(\tau)-\fp(\tau')$ is a nonzero $\K$-linear form in
$(\gamma_{\mathrm{pos}},\gamma_{\mathrm{idx}},\mu)$: if the position or
index bits differ it is nonconstant in the corresponding $\gamma$
coordinate; otherwise $v\ne v'$ and it equals $\mu(v'-v)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Multiset binding]\label{lem:multiset}
Condition on distinct fingerprints (Lemma~\ref{lem:collision}). If the
multiset $\{\!\{\tau_q\}\!\}_{q\in Q}$ differs from
$\biguplus_e \{\tau_e\ \text{with multiplicity}\ m_e\}$, then the two
sides of \eqref{eq:multiset}, as polynomials in $\delta$, are distinct,
and they agree at a random $\delta$ with probability at most
$\max(|Q|,\textstyle\sum_e m_e)/|\K| \le 2^{t+s+w+6}/2^{128}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Both sides are monic products of linear factors; by unique factorization
in $\K[\delta]$ they are equal iff their root multisets coincide with
identical \emph{integer} multiplicities. Distinct fingerprints make the
root multisets faithful images of the triple multisets. Schwartz--Zippel
gives the agreement bound. Note the multiplicities are counted in $\Z$
as factor multiplicities --- this is the point at which the argument is
sound in characteristic two, where the additive
(logarithmic-derivative-with-field-multiplicities) form
$\sum_e m_e/(\delta-\fp_e)$ would collapse even counts.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Soundness, sketch]\label{thm:sound}
Under the transcript order of \S\ref{sec:fs}, if the verifier accepts
then, except with probability
$O\!\bigl((N^2 + \mathrm{rounds})/|\K|\bigr)$ over the challenges plus
the binding errors of the commitment and opening, the committed data $D$
satisfies $y=\MLE[\INT(D)](r)\bmod q$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(i) The forest GKRs pin the claimed roots to their actual leaf wires
(standard grand-product GKR soundness over the merged layers).
(ii) The byte-driven discharge pins each leaf wire of both dense
families to the public table read at the committed octet: the case-LUT
rounds are exact algebraic identities over the committed bits, and their
terminal claims are discharged by the PCS opening; hence the supplied
values satisfy $v_q=T_{\beta_q}[a_q]$ \emph{provided} the multiset
identity holds --- and independently the fingerprint leaves equal
$G_{\beta_q}[a_q]$ as functions of the same bits, so no adaptive choice
of wires survives the discharge. (iii) By Lemmas
\ref{lem:collision}--\ref{lem:multiset}, acceptance of
\eqref{eq:multiset} forces the query multiset to equal the table
multiset, i.e.\ every query's value is the table entry at its committed
index. (iv) Therefore each value tree's root equals
$\alpha^{v_{j,c}}$ with $v_{j,c}$ the true fold of
\eqref{eq:switched}; by Lemma~\ref{lem:magnitude} and the range checks,
the clear (or committed) folds equal the true folds by injectivity of
$v\mapsto\alpha^v$ below $\ord(\alpha)$. (v) The read-off
$y=\sum_c w'_c\sum_j 2^j v_{j,c}\bmod q$ is deterministic public
arithmetic on bound integers, and equals $\MLE[\INT(D)](r)\bmod q$ by
\eqref{eq:fold}--\eqref{eq:switched}.
\end{proof}

replace (fingerprint + multiplicity) by fraction terms at measured
$\phi\approx 1.3$ and add the $Y$-binding instance
($\approx 2^{7-s}N$ committed bits). Three observations:

\emph{(a) Where the $W$ factor acts.} The prior $W{=}1$ octet design paid
a table-side factor $c_f = 1+2^{8-s}$ inside its certificate (measured
fatal at $s{=}7$, marginal at $s{=}10$); here the sharing reduces the
analogous terms to the multiplicity forest and table rows above ---
$O((s{+}w)/2^s)$ --- because the table population is $W\times$ smaller
relative to the queries. This is the only place wide-word structure
does real work, and it is worth exactly $\Theta(W)$ on those terms.

\emph{(b) $W=1$ non-applicability.} At $W=1$ there are no planes to
share across; the construction degenerates to the prior design and its
measured verdict. The advantage claimed here \emph{exists only for wide
cells} --- consistently with the project's earlier finding that no
arrangement makes $W$-blind certification cheaper.

\emph{(c) The bar.} The project's adoption bar for certificate-bearing
redesigns is a measured $\ge 1.5\times$ prover advantage with all adders
priced (extra commitments, proof bytes, verifier work, peak memory), a
bar set by the history of four predecessor designs whose op-count models
overstated measured performance --- ``the witness forest cancels the
win'' was measured four times. The model above \emph{straddles} $1.0$;
this note therefore specifies, and does not prejudge, the gate:

\begin{itemize}
\item Harness: the existing octet-gate harness extended with (i) shared
tables across planes, (ii) both certificate arms measured same-run,
(iii) the multiplicity planes and (for arm B) the $Y$ instance charged
as real committed data, (iv) the byte discharge implemented, not
modeled.
\item Shapes: genuine $W{=}32$ at $(t,s)\in\{(12,9),(12,11)\}$, run
against the current fused, bit-driven, $L{=}1$ baseline on an idle box,
medians of $\ge 3$, one shape per process.
\item Decision: adopt only if the gate clears $1.5\times$ with adders;
record the outcome either way in the project ledger.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related constructions}\label{sec:related}

Logarithmic-derivative lookups originate with LogUp \cite{logup} and its
GKR formulation \cite{logupgkr}; the multiplicity-free indexed variant
is logup* \cite{logupstar}. In characteristic two the additive form is
unsound (even multiplicities vanish); the two sound routes used here are
the multiplicative grand-product multiset (folklore; in-project
reference implementation in the \texttt{f2-clean-lookup} machinery) and
the pushforward form (in-project \texttt{quotient\_gkr.rs}). Binius
\cite{binius} certifies exponentiation by committed bits with the same
bit-affine product structure; its blueprint's exponentiation gadget
\cite{biniusblueprint} matches our baseline in the static-base case
(public base, per-exponent-bit cost, ``no windowing''), and its
Frobenius phase --- untwisting $V^{2^i}$ claims through
$\varphi^{-i}$-twisted evaluation points --- is Lemma~\ref{lem:twist},
which we need only on the verifier side (table-side exit evaluations),
having removed all in-circuit twists by Remark~\ref{rem:wraparound}.
The width-conservation accounting (Remark~\ref{rem:conservation}) and
the measured verdicts cited throughout are from the project ledger
(f2-int workstream, 2026-06/07): the $W{=}1$ octet gate, the wide-outer
tensor and weight-slicing adjudications, and the kernel-parity re-gate
of the pushforward certificate.

\section{Status}\label{sec:status}

This is a design note. Soundness (Section~\ref{sec:soundness}) we
consider settled up to the standard forest/opening arguments it inherits;
performance is a model straddling the adoption bar, to be settled by the
gate of Section~\ref{sec:costs} and by nothing else. Independent of the
gate's outcome, two elements of the analysis stand on their own: the
per-plane formulation removes weight chunking for every $t\le 27$
(subsuming the baseline's $L{=}2$ penalty at $W{=}32$), and
Remark~\ref{rem:wraparound} documents a soundness pitfall (correction-
digit wraparound) that any in-exponent aggregation design must avoid.
