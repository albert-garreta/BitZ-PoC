\begin{abstract}
\noindent
F2Z's integer-MLE opening rides on a grand-product argument: per column
$c$ of the committed bit matrix, the prover must certify
$\alpha^{u_c} = \prod_i \Lambda[c][i]$ for the \emph{bit-affine} leaves
$\Lambda[c][i] = 1 + M[c][i]\cdot\tau(i)$.  This is the prover's dominant
phase (81--96\,\% of prove time across $n = 16$--$32$), and this note
explains how it is actually performed, as implemented in
\code{src/merged\_forest.rs} and the eq-factored sumcheck driver it runs
on.  Three ideas carry the design.  (i)~\emph{Merging}: the $2^s$
product trees are one multilinear family with the tree index as extra
MLE variables, so one GKR run binds every root and each layer's payload
is $O(\ell + s)$ field elements instead of $O(2^s)$.  (ii)~\emph{The
two-phase eq-factored layer sumcheck}: phase~A binds the in-tree
variables with the trees as eq-weighted groups sharing one reduction
point; phase~B binds the tree-index variables over the per-tree finals,
which never enter the transcript.  The driver materialises no $\eq$
tables, accumulates round coefficients in unreduced $256$-bit form, and
sends each round in Gruen's factored form: the two non-constant
coefficients of the quadratic cofactor, the shared $\eq$ factor and the
constant coefficient being the verifier's to reconstruct.  (iii)~\emph{Laziness}: the leaf layer and the
bottom product levels are never built --- the bottom layers run
branchless case-LUT sumcheck rounds straight off the packed committed
bits against shared $\tau$-product tables, one intermediate level is
regenerated just in time (with its first round message fused into the
generation pass), and the stored chain tops out a quarter (default
schedule) or an eighth (\code{l8}) of the way up the tree, which is what
caps prover memory at ${\approx}\,2^n{\cdot}4$\,B resp.\
${\approx}\,2^n{\cdot}2$\,B.  Every lazy path is an exact
characteristic-2 rewriting of the eager forest: the transcripts are
byte-identical, and tests pin this in every schedule.  The note closes
with the exit-claim discharge into the ring-switch/Ligerito opening, the
batched and RLC-family variants of the forest, and the measured cost
tables.
\end{abstract}

\section{The job of the forest}
\label{sec:job}

F2Z\footnote{Repository \code{f2z-pcs}, a standalone extraction of the
\code{f2-int} pipeline from \code{zinc-plus} (branch
\code{f2-int-unified-ligerito}); the construction is due to Lev Soukhanov
(the char2-fieldswitch note, \S9).  \code{docs/DESIGN.md} holds the
distilled protocol; \code{docs/verifier-note/} specifies every verifier
check; \code{docs/rlc-family-note/} develops the experimental claim
families.  This note is the \emph{prover-side} companion: how the forest
is computed, scheduled, and kept small.} proves an \emph{integer}
multilinear-extension evaluation
$\smash{\mle{\INT(D)}}(r) = y \in \Fq$ over data $D$ whose \emph{bits}
are committed with a characteristic-2 commitment.  The instance shape is
$(t, s, W)$ (\code{IntEvalParams}): $D$ is a $2^t \times 2^s$ matrix of
$W$-bit cells, its bits arranged as the single-bit matrix
\[
  M[c][i] \in \bits, \qquad
  i = (b \ll \log_2 W)\,|\,j \;\in\; \bits^{d}, \qquad
  d = t + \log_2 W ,
\]
with $b$ the row, $c$ the column, $j$ the bit position inside the cell.
The $t$ row variables are folded with integer weights, the $s$ column
variables are read off in the clear.  We write $n = t + s$ for the
benchmark-scale parameter (the deployed shapes have $W = 1$, so $2^n$ is
the committed bit count) and $\K = \mathrm{GF}(2^{128})$ for the binding
field, with $\alpha \in \Kx$ a generator.

\paragraph{The claim the forest certifies.}
An integer fold cannot be read through the $\Ftwo$ commitment (parities
destroy magnitudes), so F2Z carries it in the exponent of $\K$: for one
chunk $l$ of the mod-$q$ opening, with public $\cw$-bit chunk weights
$W^{(l)}_b$ (limbs of the $\Fq$ row-$\eq$ weights,
$\cw = 127 - t - W$), the prover sends the per-column integer folds
\[
  u^{(l)}_c \;=\; \sum_b W^{(l)}_b \cdot \INT\!\big(D(b,c)\big)
  \;<\; 2^{\cw + t + W} = 2^{127},
\]
and must certify them against the commitment.  The certificate is a
grand product: each factor is \emph{bit-affine} in one committed bit,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leaves}
  \Lambda^{(l)}[c][i] \;=\; 1 + M[c][i]\cdot\tau^{(l)}(i),
  \qquad
  \tau^{(l)}(i) \;=\; \alpha^{W^{(l)}_b 2^j} - 1
  \quad (i = (b \ll \log_2 W)|j),
\end{equation}
so that
$\prod_{i} \Lambda^{(l)}[c][i] = \alpha^{u^{(l)}_c}$.  (In $\K$,
$-1 = +1$; we keep the $-1$ form for readability.)  The forest's output
is a reduction of all $2^s$ product claims to \emph{one} evaluation
claim on the leaf MLE $\smash{\mle{\Lambda}}$, which
\S\ref{sec:discharge} converts into a $\K$-linear functional of the
committed bits and hands to the ring-switch + Ligerito opener.  Range
checks on the sent $u$'s and the generator property of $\alpha$ make the
exponent map injective on the values that occur, so the products bind
the integers; that chain of checks is the verifier note's
\S\S5--7, and we do not repeat it here.

\paragraph{Why performance of this phase is the whole game.}
Time is forest-dominated: the \code{forest+presum} phase is 81\,\% of
prove at $n = 16$, rising to 92--96\,\% at $n \ge 26$
(\S\ref{sec:costs}).  Proof \emph{bytes}, by contrast, are dominated by
the Ligerito opening --- the forest side stays at 8--51\,KiB through
$n = 28$.  So the forest is engineered for prover time and memory, and
its transcript is engineered to stay small; both axes are visible in the
design below.

\paragraph{Scope.}
Everything in this note is the code of this repository: the merged
forest (\code{src/merged\_forest.rs}), the eq-factored driver
(\code{src/piop/sumcheck/eq\_factored.rs}), and the table builders
(\code{src/pcs.rs}).  Function names are given so claims can be checked
against source; line numbers are avoided (the files drift).  The
Ligerito opener is out of scope (black box, as in the verifier note).

\section{One forest, one transcript: the merged statement}
\label{sec:merged}

A naive realisation runs one GKR product tree per column and carries,
per tree per layer, a pair of child evaluations --- $2\cdot 2^s\cdot d$
$\K$-elements of proof, ${\approx}512$\,KiB at $n = 26$.  The merged
forest removes the $2^s$ factor from the payload by treating the tree
index as \emph{more MLE variables}.

\paragraph{The layer family.}
Index the values of tree $c$ at depth $\ell$ (the root is depth $0$,
leaves depth $d$) by $x \in \bits^\ell$, and define one multilinear
family per depth,
\[
  L_\ell(x, c), \qquad (x, c) \in \bits^\ell \times \bits^s,
\]
with $x$ occupying the \emph{low} index bits and $c$ the high ones.
The product recursion pairs children on the \emph{top} in-tree bit:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recursion}
  L_\ell(x, c) \;=\; L_{\ell+1}(x, 0, c)\cdot L_{\ell+1}(x, 1, c),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the two children of node $x$ sit at positions $x$ and
$x + 2^{\ell}$ of level $\ell + 1$.  This convention is load-bearing for
the lazy schedules: every level is stored and consumed as a top-bit
split pair of halves $(E, O)$ ---
$E_c(x) = L_{\ell+1}(x, 0, c)$, $O_c(x) = L_{\ell+1}(x, 1, c)$ --- and
the leaf halves are exactly ``first $2^{d-1}$ bits, last $2^{d-1}$
bits'' of a column, which is what lets pair products be table lookups
(\S\ref{sec:lazy}).  $L_0(\cdot, c) = R_c$ are the roots and
$L_d = \Lambda$ the leaves.

\paragraph{Entry: the roots are derived, absorbed, and evaluated ---
never sent per-tree.}
The prover's product-tree build produces the roots $R_c$ as a byproduct;
the proof object carries no root field.  On the verifier's side the
roots are \emph{recomputed} as $R_c = \alpha^{u_c}$ from the sent folds
(fixed-base comb, \code{FixedBasePow}) --- for an honest prover these
are the same values, and that equality is precisely the claim being
proven.  Both sides then perform the same two transcript actions
(\code{drive\_grouped} / \code{verify\_merged\_forest}):
\[
  \textbf{absorb}(\mathtt{0x30}: R_0, \dots, R_{2^s-1}),
  \qquad
  \zeta \leftarrow \textbf{squeeze}(\K^s),
\]
and each computes the entry claim itself,
$C_0 = \mle{R}(\zeta)$ (\code{mle\_at}, $O(2^s)$ work).  The absorb is
the binding: a prover whose subsequent messages were generated against
different roots faces challenges derived from \emph{these} roots.  Note
the prover-side integer folds $u_c$ (\code{fold\_values\_bits}, a
popcount-style pass over the packed rows) are computed independently of
the forest and enter the proof stream as the $\code{us}$ vector; the
forest itself never touches them.

\paragraph{Per-layer payload.}
Each of the $d$ layers contributes one \code{MergedLayer} to the proof:
an optional phase-A sumcheck ($\ell$ rounds, absent at $\ell = 0$), a
phase-B sumcheck ($s$ rounds), and a closing child pair --- $O(\ell+s)$
$\K$-elements.  Summed over layers this is
${\approx}\,32\big(\tfrac{d(d-1)}{2} + ds\big)$ bytes of round messages
plus $32d$ bytes of pairs (a degree-3 round carries the two 16-byte
non-constant coefficients of its quadratic cofactor --- Gruen's factored
form, \S\ref{sec:driver}); at the deployed shapes the
\emph{sent folds} $16\cdot 2^s$\,B are the larger forest-side item
(e.g.\ $n = 28$, $s = 11$: 32\,KiB of folds vs.\ ${\approx}11$\,KiB of
forest messages, ${\approx}45.7$\,KiB forest-side total).

\section{The layer protocol}
\label{sec:layers}

The verifier maintains a running claim $C_\ell = \mle{L_\ell}(z_x, z_c)$
starting from $(z_x, z_c) = (\varepsilon, \zeta)$.  By
\eqref{eq:recursion} and multilinearity, the layer-$\ell$ claim factors
coordinate-wise:
\begin{align}
  C_\ell
  \;&=\; \sum_{x \in \bits^\ell}\sum_{c \in \bits^s}
        \eq(x, z_x)\,\eq(c, z_c)\;
        \underbrace{L_{\ell+1}(x,0,c)}_{E_c(x)}\,
        \underbrace{L_{\ell+1}(x,1,c)}_{O_c(x)} \notag\\
  \;&=\; \sum_{c} \eq(c, z_c)
        \Big[\sum_{x} \eq(x, z_x)\, E_c(x)\, O_c(x)\Big].
        \label{eq:layer}
\end{align}
The bracketed inner sums share the point $z_x$ and differ only in the
tree; that shape is exactly one \emph{shared-$q$ multi-group} instance
of the eq-factored driver (\S\ref{sec:driver}), and the layer is
discharged in two phases plus a line step
(\code{drive\_grouped}):

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Phase A} (in-tree variables; $\ell \ge 1$ only).  The
  $2^s$ trees enter as groups: group $c$ has the shared eq point
  $q = z_x$, the per-group scale $\eq(c, z_c)$ (a precomputed
  $O(2^s)$ table re-built per layer), and the single buffer pair
  $(E_c, O_c)$.  One driver call
  (\code{prove\_eq\_inner\_sumcheck\_mixed\_pre}) runs $\ell$ rounds of
  a degree-3 sumcheck binding $x \to r_x \in \K^\ell$, and returns the
  per-tree finals
  $\big(\mle{E_c}(r_x), \mle{O_c}(r_x)\big)$.  These finals are
  \emph{prover-internal}: nothing per-tree is absorbed at any point.
\item \textbf{Phase B} (tree-index variables).  The residual
  $\sum_c \eq(c, z_c)\,\mle{E_c}(r_x)\,\mle{O_c}(r_x)$ is itself an
  eq-weighted inner product over $c$ --- the per-tree finals, read as
  two length-$2^s$ multilinear tables $E(\cdot), O(\cdot)$, feed one
  more (tiny, dense) driver call with $q = z_c$, binding
  $c \to r_c \in \K^s$ and closing on the child pair
  \[
    (p, q) \;=\;
    \big(\mle{L_{\ell+1}}(r_x, 0, r_c),\;
         \mle{L_{\ell+1}}(r_x, 1, r_c)\big).
  \]
\item \textbf{Line step.}  $\textbf{absorb}(\mathtt{0x32}: p, q)$,
  $\lambda_\ell \leftarrow \textbf{squeeze}(\K)$, and the claim advances
  along the line through the two children:
  \[
    C_{\ell+1} \;=\; p + \lambda_\ell\,(p + q), \qquad
    z_x \leftarrow r_x\,\|\,\lambda_\ell, \qquad
    z_c \leftarrow r_c .
  \]
  (The code calls $\lambda_\ell$ \code{mu}; renamed to keep $\mu$ for
  the discharge residual, as in the verifier note.)
\end{enumerate}

Layer $0$ degenerates: there are no in-tree variables yet, phase A is
skipped (\code{sc\_x = None}), and the pair vectors fed to phase B are
simply each root's two children --- level $1$, two values per tree.
After $d$ layers the \emph{exit claim} is
\[
  e_d \;=\; C_d \;\overset{\text{honest}}{=}\;
  \mle{\Lambda}(z_x, z_c),
  \qquad z = (z_x, z_c) \in \K^{d+s},
\]
pinned by the \code{merged\_forest\_roundtrips} test against
\code{mle\_at(leaves, $z$)}.  The verifier-side chaining checks (phase-A
claimed sum $= C_\ell$, the factored-$\eq$ linkage
$E_A = \eq(r_x, z_x)\cdot\code{sc\_c.claimed\_sum}$, the closing check
$E_B = \eq(r_c, z_c)\cdot p\cdot q$) are [V5]--[V11] of the verifier
note and mirror this structure exactly.

\begin{remark}[Why two phases]
A single $(\ell + s)$-variable sumcheck over the merged index would be
sound too, but would need the $\eq(c, z_c)$ weights woven into every
round over a buffer of size $2^{\ell + s}$.  The split lets phase A
treat $\eq(c, z_c)$ as a per-group \emph{scalar} (it seeds the group's
prefix scalar, \S\ref{sec:driver}, at zero marginal cost), keeps the
per-tree buffers independent --- which is what the lazy bit-driven
rounds and the across-tree parallelism need --- and makes phase B
negligible ($2^s$-sized buffers).  The transcript cost is one extra
sumcheck header per layer.
\end{remark}

\section{The eq-factored driver}
\label{sec:driver}

Every phase of every layer runs on one prover,
\code{prove\_eq\_inner\_sumcheck\_mixed\_pre}
(\code{src/piop/sumcheck/eq\_factored.rs}), which proves sums of the
shape
\[
  \sum_{x} \sum_{g} \eq(x; q_g)\cdot \mathrm{scale}_g \cdot
  L_g(x)\,R_g(x)
\]
--- an eq-weighted sum of inner products of multilinear pairs --- in one
of two round-message formats.  In its \emph{generic} format the driver
is \textbf{byte-identical} to the generic \code{MLSumcheck} prover run
over the materialised comb $[\eq_1, \dots, L\text{'s}, R\text{'s}]$ with
the degree-3 combiner $\sum_g \eq_g\, L_g R_g$: same round polynomials,
same transcript operations (header, tail absorbs, post-draw challenge
re-absorb), same proof layout, and the generic verifier verifies it
unchanged.  The forest's layers (all groups at one shared point) instead
run the \emph{Gruen} format (2026-08-15): two coefficients per round
instead of three tail evaluations, verified by a dedicated
${\sim}$20-line coefficient-form round check
(\code{verify\_eq\_inner\_sumcheck\_gruen}); see the
coefficient-accumulation paragraph below.  The RLC-discharge cascades,
whose groups sit at \emph{different} points, keep the generic format.

\paragraph{Never materialise $\eq$.}
Round $j$ (binding the \emph{low} variable, as the generic prover does)
writes each group's round message as
\[
  P_j(c) \;=\; \sum_g A_{g,j}\;\eq_1\!\big(c;\, q_g[j-1]\big)\;
  H_{g,j}(c),
  \qquad
  H_{g,j}(c) \;=\; \sum_b V_{g,j}[b]\; L_g(c, b)\, R_g(c, b),
\]
where $L_g(c, b) = L_g[2b] + c\,(L_g[2b{+}1] - L_g[2b])$ is the
fold-in-progress buffer at suffix slot $b$, and the $\eq$ weight has
been split into three parts, none of them a materialised multiplicand:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
\item the \emph{prefix scalar}
  $A_{g,j} = \mathrm{scale}_g\cdot\prod_{i<j-1}\eq_1(\rho_{i+1};
  q_g[i])$, one running $\K$-element per group, updated per round ---
  this is where phase A's $\eq(c, z_c)$ group scale rides for free;
\item the current-variable factor $\eq_1(c; q_g[j-1])$, degree 1 in the
  round variable;
\item the \emph{suffix tensor}
  $V_{g,j}[b] = \prod_{i\ge j}\eq_1(b_{i-j}; q_g[i])$, precomputed
  back-to-front for all rounds in $O(2^k)$ total (\code{suffix\_tensors})
  --- the cost of one $\eq$ build, and built \emph{once} for the whole
  phase-A call, since all trees share $q = z_x$ (asserted:
  bit-selected groups require the shared point).
\end{itemize}
$H$ is quadratic and the $\eq_1$ factor linear, so $P_j$ has degree 3,
matching the pinned verifier degree.

\paragraph{Coefficient-space accumulation, wide sums, two-element
messages.}
The per-slot inner loop accumulates the \emph{coefficients}
$(A_0, A_1, A_2)$ of $H$ in the round variable rather than its node
values, so no per-node multiplies enter the hot loop; the
single-pair case costs 5 field multiplies per slot (the suffix weight is
folded into $L$: $w{\cdot}l_0$, $w{\cdot}l_1$, then three products
$\smash{(w l_0)r_0}$, $\smash{(w l_1)r_1}$,
$\Delta L\cdot\Delta R$).  The three products feed
\code{WideMulAcc} accumulators: unreduced 256-bit carry-less sums with
\emph{one} polynomial reduction per accumulator per round --- exact,
because the reduction is $\Ftwo$-linear.  In the Gruen format
\cite{Gruen24} the coefficients are also what is \emph{sent}: with the
shared point, $P_j(X) = \eq_1(X; q[j{-}1])\cdot\hat H_j(X)$ where
$\hat H_j = \sum_g A_{g,j} H_{g,j}$, and the round message is the pair
$(\hat H_1, \hat H_2)$ --- the polynomial is never evaluated at any
node.  The verifier reconstructs
$\hat H_0 = S_j - q[j{-}1]\,(\hat H_1 + \hat H_2)$ from the chained sum
$S_j = P_j(0) + P_j(1)$ (an identity over any field --- the
$(1{-}q)\hat H_0 + q\hat H_0$ cross terms collapse; no inversions) and
advances $S_{j+1} = \eq_1(\rho_j; q[j{-}1])\cdot\hat H_j(\rho_j)$.
A cheating prover is thereby restricted to degree-3 polynomials
divisible by the public $\eq_1$ factor --- a subset of the generic
message space, so the standard round analysis applies unchanged.
(The generic format instead converts the triple to the four Lagrange
nodes $\{0, 1, X, X{+}1\}$ and sends three tails; in characteristic 2
those nodes form an $\Ftwo$-affine 2-flat over which every quadratic
sums to zero, so the fourth node is the sum of the other three.)  On
aarch64 the whole-buffer message and fold passes dispatch to fused NEON
kernels (\code{eqf\_single\_pair\_round} and friends;
\code{F2Z\_EQF\_NOKERNEL=1} forces the generic loops for A/B
diagnosis).

\paragraph{Pass fusion.}
By default the driver defers each round's fold and runs it \emph{fused
into the next round's message pass}: one read of the unfolded buffers
replaces a fold-write pass plus a message-read pass.  Measured
${\approx}10\,\%$ of prove at $n = 26$--$28$; byte-identical (same field
values in the same transcript order --- only the physical pass structure
changes); \code{F2Z\_EQF\_FUSE=0} restores the eager two-pass path.
The same idea appears one level up in the forest's JIT layers: the
buffer-\emph{generation} pass accumulates the round-1 coefficients as it
writes (\code{dense\_jit\_fused\_round1}, forwarded through the driver's
\code{pre\_round1} hook), so a JIT-generated level is written once and
first \emph{read} by round 2's fused fold+message pass
(\code{F2Z\_JIT\_R1=0} opts out).  XOR accumulation is order-free and
reduction $\Ftwo$-linear, hence generation-order independence of the
reduced coefficients --- the fusion is again an exact identity.

\section{Leaves from bits: the lazy schedules}
\label{sec:lazy}

The eager reference prover (\code{prove\_merged\_forest}) materialises
the $2^{d+s}$ leaves and every product level above them.  At the
deployed shapes that is not a constant factor away from affordable: at
$n = 28$ ($d = 17$, $s = 11$) the leaf layer alone is $2^{28}$
$\K$-elements $=4$\,GiB, the level above it 2\,GiB, the whole forest
${\approx}8$\,GiB --- whereas the measured peak resident set of an
\emph{entire} $n = 28$ prove is 1.27\,GB on the 16\,GB M4 of
\S\ref{sec:costs}.  The lazy prover
(\code{prove\_merged\_forest\_lazy}) produces a byte-identical
transcript while never materialising the leaf layer or the bottom
product levels at all.

Everything rests on one observation: near the leaves the forest's values
carry almost no entropy.  A node $r$ levels above the leaves is the
product of the $2^r$ leaves beneath it, and every leaf is either $1$ or
a public table entry \eqref{eq:leaves} --- so the node's value is one of
$2^{2^r}$ possibilities, \emph{selected by $2^r$ committed bits}, out of
a table that depends on the node's position but not on its tree.  For
small $r$ that replaces ``compute and store $2^s$ copies of the level''
by ``gather from one shared table indexed by committed bits''.
\S\ref{sec:square} turns the observation into the two inequalities that
generate every variant in the code, \S\ref{sec:tables} lists what the
prover actually holds, \S\ref{sec:ladder} assigns every level of the
tree to a sourcing mode, \S\ref{sec:lutrounds} works through the round
bodies, and \S\ref{sec:identity} says why the result is the same
protocol.

One index convention first, because it ties the two halves of this
section together: layer $\ell$'s phase A binds the $\ell$ in-tree
variables over level $\ell + 1$, which has $2^\ell$ values per half ---
so \emph{layer $\ell$ is a $k = \ell$ variable instance of the driver},
and the bottom three layers of a depth-$d$ tree run at
$k = d-3,\, d-2,\, d-1$.  Every ``$k \ge \cdot$'' guard below is a
statement about depth.

\subsection{Two square laws and one cap}
\label{sec:square}

Two independent operations widen the case tables the lazy prover reads,
and both \emph{square} the number of cases.  The shape of the whole
prover follows from where their common cap bites.

\begin{identity}[Level width]\label{id:level}
Unfolding the recursion \eqref{eq:recursion} $r$ times, position $y$ of
level $d - r$ in tree $c$ is
\[
  L_{d-r}(y, c) \;=\;
  \prod_{\varepsilon \in \bits^{r}}
    \Lambda[c]\Big[\, y + \sum_{i=1}^{r}\varepsilon_i\, 2^{d-i}\Big] ,
\]
the product over the $2^r$ leaves whose low $d-r$ index bits are $y$
(the top $r$ bits run free).  With $\Lambda[c][i] \in \{1, v(i)\}$,
$v(i) = \alpha^{W_b 2^j}$, that value is one of $2^{2^r}$
position-indexed constants, selected by the $2^r$ committed bits of the
subcube --- and the constants do not depend on $c$.
\end{identity}

A level-$(d{-}r)$ case table therefore holds
$2^{2^r}\!\cdot 2^{d-r}$ entries: $2^{d+1}$, $2^{d+1}$, $2^{d+2}$,
$2^{d+5}$ for $r = 0, 1, 2, 3$, i.e.\ 4\,MiB, 4\,MiB, 8\,MiB,
\textbf{128\,MiB} at $d = 17$.  The first three are built once, shared
across all $2^s = 2048$ trees, and stay cache-resident; the fourth does
not exist in the code, and that 16-fold jump is why.

\begin{identity}[Fold width]\label{id:fold}
A sumcheck round replaces the entry pair $(v_{2p}, v_{2p+1})$ by
$v'_p = v_{2p} + \rho\,(v_{2p+1} - v_{2p})$.  If each $v$ was a
$2^w$-case function of $w$ bits at its own position, then $v'_p$ is a
$2^{2w}$-case function of the $2w$ bits of the two positions --- again a
per-position table (now $\rho$-dependent), again shared by all trees.
\end{identity}

So an entry of level $d - r$ with $f$ variables already bound is a
$2^{2^{r+f}}$-case select on $2^{r+f}$ bits, and the same
``at most 4 selecting bits'' cap reads $r + f \le 2$.  A layer whose
input level is $d - r$ can therefore run $3 - r$ rounds straight off the
bits; and a prefix of $f$ such rounds needs $k \ge f + 1$ variables,
because the last of them must still have a fold --- that fold is where
the dense buffers are born.  Those two inequalities generate the
driver's entire \code{GroupBufs} inventory, its assertions, and the
depth fallbacks of \S\ref{sec:ladder}:

\subsection{What the prover holds}
\label{sec:tables}

\paragraph{Bits, transposed once.}
The commitment hint stores the bit matrix column-lane--packed: word $i$
of group $g$ carries bit $i$ of the 64 columns $64g \dots 64g{+}63$.
The forest wants the opposite --- one contiguous bit array per tree ---
so a single pass (\code{extract\_column\_bit\_halves}) changes the
layout with $64{\times}64$ in-register bit transposes
(\code{transpose64} of 64 consecutive position-words yields, in lane
$j$, the position-packed word of column $64g{+}j$) and writes each tree's
bits already split at the midpoint:
\[
  \code{lbits}_c[i] = M[c][i], \qquad
  \code{rbits}_c[i] = M[c][i + 2^{d-1}], \qquad i < 2^{d-1},
\]
packed 64 per \code{u64} --- $2^d/8$ bytes per tree, $2^n/8$ = 32\,MiB
for the whole matrix at $n = 28$.  These two arrays drive \emph{every}
lazy layer of tree $c$'s bottom.  The split is not a convenience: by the
top-bit convention of \S\ref{sec:merged}, the free index bits of
Identity~\ref{id:level} are the \emph{high} ones, so the top leaf-index
bit of a subcube selects the array and the remaining $r-1$ free bits are
position offsets \emph{inside} it.  Concretely, a level-$(d{-}1)$ node at
position $i$ multiplies leaves $i$ and $i + 2^{d-1}$, and its two
selecting bits are bit $i$ of \code{lbits} and bit $i$ of \code{rbits}
--- the same index into both arrays; a level-$(d{-}2)$ node at position
$y$ takes bits $y$ and $y + 2^{d-2}$ of both.  Sweeping bits word-wise
therefore costs 4 \code{u64} loads per 64 values, ${\approx}64\times$
less read traffic than per-bit \code{packed\_col\_bit} gathers over the
lane-packed store, which touch a whole word per bit.

\paragraph{$\alpha$-power chains.}
Per chunk, \code{chunk\_pow2\_table} builds
$v(i) = \alpha^{W^{(l)}_b 2^j}$ for all $i = (b \ll \log_2 W)|j$.  All
$2^t$ rows share one $\alpha$-squaring chain, so the row bases
$\alpha^{W_b}$ come from a single fixed-base comb (\code{FixedBasePow},
window 8 --- ${\approx}8\times$ fewer multiplies than per-row
square-and-multiply), and a row's $W$ place values are then its own
squaring chain $\alpha^{w2^j} = (\alpha^{w2^{j-1}})^2$.  Every table
below is built from this one array.

\paragraph{The four shared table families.}
Built once per weight set (``$\tau$ set'') and read by all $2^s$ trees.
Write $q_1 = 2^{d-2}$, $q_2 = 2^{d-1}$.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{leaf $\tau$ halves} (\code{leaf\_tau\_halves}):
  $\tau(i) = v(i) + 1$, split like the bits.  This is the $r = 0$ table
  in minimal form --- case $0$ is the constant $1$ and needs no storage,
  so one entry per position suffices and the leaf is recovered as the
  bit-affine $1 + m\,\tau$ of \eqref{eq:leaves}.  (Padded rows have
  $v = \alpha^0 = 1$, hence $\tau = 0$: their leaves are $1$ whatever
  bits they carry.)
\item the \emph{pair table} (\code{layer1\_pair\_table}):
  $\code{pair}[i] = v(i)\,v(i + q_2)$ for $i < q_2$ --- the
  both-bits-set case of a level-$(d{-}1)$ node, and the only one of its
  four cases that is not already an entry of $v$.  Building it once
  instead of once per column saves, at random bit density, a quarter of
  all pairs times $2^s$ columns of redundant $\K$-multiplies.
\item the \emph{$r = 1$ case tables} \code{te}, \code{to}: for $y < q_1$,
  \[
    \code{te}[4y + c] = \big(1,\; v(y),\; v(y{+}q_2),\;
      \code{pair}[y]\big)_c , \qquad
    \code{to}[4y + c] = \text{the same at } y + q_1 ,
  \]
  with case index $c = m_{\mathrm{lo}} \,|\, m_{\mathrm{hi}}{\ll}1$
  ($\mathrm{lo}$ = the first-half leaf, $\mathrm{hi}$ = the second-half
  one).  \code{te} covers the E half of level $d{-}1$ (position $y$,
  leaves $y,\,y{+}q_2$), \code{to} the O half (position $y{+}q_1$,
  leaves $y{+}q_1,\,y{+}q_1{+}q_2$) --- the offset is baked into the
  table, so both are read at the same index $y$.
\item the \emph{$r = 2$ table} $T4$:
  $T4[(y \ll 4)|(c_E \ll 2)|c_O] =
   \code{te}[4y{+}c_E]\cdot\code{to}[4y{+}c_O]$ --- all 16 values of the
  level-$(d{-}2)$ node at $y$, which pairs level-$(d{-}1)$ positions $y$
  and $y + q_1$, i.e.\ the four leaves
  $y,\; y{+}q_1,\; y{+}q_2,\; y{+}q_1{+}q_2$, selected by four committed
  bits.
\end{itemize}
Each family costs $O(2^d)$ per set and is amortised $2^s$-fold.  The
$T4$ build collects an indexed \code{Vec<[Gf; 16]>} and flattens it in
place: a parallel nested \code{flatten} treats every 16-entry row as its
own parallel iterator and makes the collect unindexed --- measured
${\approx}836\times$ slower than the same arithmetic run serially
(upstream fix \code{e19b0e1}).

\paragraph{Sizes.}
Everything the lazy prover holds, with the $n = 28$ instantiation
($d = 17$, $s = 11$, $W = 1$):

\subsection{The level ladder}
\label{sec:ladder}

Layer $\ell$'s phase A consumes level $\ell + 1$, so the prover must
decide, for each of the $d$ levels, how it comes into existence.  There
are three answers.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Stored} --- built once bottom-up by \code{build\_levels}
  and held level-major until its layer runs.  Each tree's chain is built
  independently (parallel across trees) by repeated
  \code{parent\_halves\_top} --- $v[i] = E[i]\cdot O[i]$, re-split at the
  new midpoint --- then the chains are regrouped level-major and the
  roots fall out of level 1.  \code{drive\_grouped} takes each level with
  \code{mem::take} as its layer starts, so the chain shrinks as the
  forest climbs.
\item \textbf{JIT} --- regenerated from bits and tables at the start of
  its own layer, consumed by that layer's rounds, dropped.
\item \textbf{Implicit} --- never $\K$-values at all: the layer's first
  one, two or three rounds run case-LUT bodies straight off the bits
  (\S\ref{sec:lutrounds}), and the first dense buffers to appear are
  already folded ones.
\end{itemize}
The interesting level is $d - 2$.  It is the highest level a
$\le 16$-case table still reaches (Identity~\ref{id:level}) and the
largest level whose layer could be driven from bits, and at
$2^{n-2}$ values it is also the biggest thing the prover would ever
store.  The three schedules are precisely the three answers to ``what
happens to level $d-2$'', and they differ in nothing else:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
 & L/2 (\code{l2}) & L/4 (default) & L/8 (\code{l8}) \\
\midrule
chain tops at        & level $d{-}2$ & level $d{-}3$ & level $d{-}4$ \\
per position of the top & 1 $T4$ gather & 2 gathers, 1 mul & 4 gathers, 3 muls \\
level $d{-}2$        & stored & JIT at layer $d{-}3$ & implicit (\code{T4Bits}) \\
level $d{-}3$        & stored & stored & JIT at layer $d{-}4$ \\
layer $d{-}2$ (pair) & \multicolumn{3}{c}{\code{Pair3Bits} over $(\code{te}, \code{to})$} \\
layer $d{-}1$ (leaf) & \multicolumn{3}{c}{\code{Leaf3Bits} over $\tau$} \\
peak resident        & ${\approx}2^n{\cdot}8$\,B & ${\approx}2^n{\cdot}4$\,B
                     & ${\approx}2^n{\cdot}2$\,B \\
needs depth          & $\ge 4$ & $\ge 4$ & $\ge 5$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{The build.}
\code{gen\_top} produces the chain's top level directly from the bits and
$T4$, one subcube of Identity~\ref{id:level} per position: L/2 reads one
$T4$ entry ($=$ level $d{-}2$), L/4 multiplies the pair
$\{y,\, y + 2^{d-3}\}$ ($=$ level $d{-}3$), L/8 the quadruple
$\{y,\, y{+}2^{d-4},\, y{+}2^{d-3},\, y{+}3\cdot2^{d-4}\}$ ($=$ level
$d{-}4$).  Levels $d,\,d{-}1$ and (below L/2) $d{-}2$ therefore never
exist at build time: each of their positions is gathered exactly once,
with the materialise-then-read round trip removed.  The chain that
remains sums to $\sum_{\ell \le \mathrm{top}} 2^\ell \approx
2^{\mathrm{top}+1}$ values per tree --- a quarter of the leaf count
under L/4, an eighth under L/8, whence the names.

\paragraph{The JIT layer.}
Under L/4, layer $d-3$ regenerates level $d-2$ per tree ($2^{d-3}$
values per half, one $T4$ gather each) into \code{Dense} buffers that
die when the layer ends; under L/8, layer $d-4$ does the same for level
$d-3$ at two gathers and a multiply per value.  By default that
generation pass is not just a write: \code{jit\_layer\_generate}
accumulates the layer's opening round(s) \emph{as it writes}, so the
level is never re-read for a message pass it could have paid for during
generation.  With the driver's double-fold on
(\code{F2Z\_EQF\_DOUBLE}, default) and $k \ge 3$, the pass accumulates
the whole $3{\times}3$ bivariate grid (\code{dense\_jit\_fused\_grid}):
nine field elements per tree, from which the driver derives \emph{both}
round 1 and round 2's messages, so its first read of the level is round
3's pass --- which then folds two deferred challenges at once.
Otherwise the pass accumulates round 1's coefficient triple only
(\code{dense\_jit\_fused\_round1}).  Either way the result reaches the
driver through its \code{pre\_round1} hook and is value-exact against
the message pass it replaces: the same weight-folds and wide products,
XOR-accumulated (order-free) and reduced once, the reduction being
$\Ftwo$-linear.  \code{F2Z\_JIT\_GRID=0} keeps the triple,
\code{F2Z\_JIT\_R1=0} the plain write.

\paragraph{Shallow trees.}
The guards of \S\ref{sec:square} at $k = \ell$ give the fallback ladder
directly, and the code takes exactly these branches:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
depth $d$ & what runs & the binding constraint \\
\midrule
$< 3$ & the eager prover over dense leaves
      & any LUT round needs $k \ge 2$ \\
$3$   & chain from level $d{-}1$, then \code{LeafBits}
      & \code{Pair2Bits} needs $k = d{-}2 \ge 2$ \\
$4$   & L/4 (forced), \code{Pair2Bits} + \code{Leaf2Bits}
      & \code{Leaf3Bits} needs $k = d{-}1 \ge 4$ \\
$\ge 5$ & the full ladder above & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
At depth 3 the chain's top level is built by
\code{build\_column\_layer1\_halves}, a zero-multiply case copy that
reads the $r = 1$ cases straight out of $v$ and \code{pair} rather than
through the driver.  At depth 4 the L/8 request degenerates (its
\code{T4Bits} layer would need $k = d-3 \ge 2$) and falls back to L/4.
\code{F2Z\_LUT3=0} forces the shallower \code{Pair2Bits}/\code{Leaf2Bits}
prefixes at every depth --- the same code path the depth-4 edge takes.

\paragraph{Only the live columns are built.}
A committed column whose bits are all zero has every leaf $\alpha^0 = 1$,
hence every level and its root $= 1$: a full $2^d$-value pipeline
(bit extraction, $T4$ gathers, JIT regeneration, one dense group per
layer) to prove a constant.  Since a phase-A round message is
$\sum_c \eq(c, z_c)\,H_c(\cdot)$ with $H_c$ linear in the group's scale,
and every constant-$1$ tree has the \emph{same} $H_c$, the trailing run
of zero columns collapses into \emph{one} synthetic group with all-ones
buffers and scale $\sum_{c \ge \mathit{live}} \eq(c, z_c)$
(\code{col\_elide}, \code{live\_cols}; the absorbed root vector is still
padded with the known $1$s, so the statement is unchanged, and on a JIT
layer the synthetic group is born inside the generation pass, since the
driver cannot recompute a fused round-1 entry itself).  Char-2 addition
is XOR and re-association is exact, so the transcript is byte-identical
to the un-elided forest (\code{col\_elision\_matches\_full}): a witness
of $N$ cells padded to $2^n$ pays the forest for
$\lceil N/2^{t + \log_2 W}\rceil$ columns instead of $2^s$.  Everything
in this section runs over the \code{live} leading trees only.

\begin{remark}[Why the ladder stops at L/4]
Going \emph{down} the ladder buys memory cheaply: L/8 costs a measured
$+5$--$13\,\%$ of prove at the 2-column model shapes, and is what makes
$n \ge 31$ possible on a 16\,GB box (\S\ref{sec:costs}).  Going
\emph{up} does not pay symmetrically.  L/2 removes a whole $T4$ sweep
over every tree --- the JIT regeneration --- but doubles the resident
set, and measured across three shapes including a deep proxy at
$4\times$ L2 it comes out flat to worse than L/4.  The reason is a
useful rule of thumb for this prover: one $T4$ gather sweep costs about
one streaming pass over the level it would have saved, so the choice of
schedule is a near-pure \emph{memory} decision with a near-zero time
gradient.  The time levers live inside the round bodies, not in the
ladder.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Inside the case-LUT rounds}
\label{sec:lutrounds}

Fix a layer with $k = \ell$ variables.  Round $j$ binds the low variable
over $2^{k-j}$ slots; slot $b$ owns entries $2b$ and $2b{+}1$ of each
side and contributes to the three coefficients of \S\ref{sec:driver},
\[
  A_0 = \sum_b w_b L[2b]R[2b], \qquad
  A_2 = \sum_b w_b\,\Delta L_b\,\Delta R_b, \qquad
  A_1 = \Big(\sum_b w_b L[2b{+}1]R[2b{+}1]\Big) + A_0 + A_2 ,
\]
with $w_b = V_j[b]$ the suffix-tensor weight and
$\Delta L_b = L[2b{+}1] + L[2b]$ (char 2, so $+ = -$; the $A_1$ form is
the char-2 reading of $A_1 = H(1) - A_0 - A_2$).  A bit-driven round
computes these sums \emph{without the $L$, $R$ arrays existing}: the
entries are per-position case selects, so each slot's three
contributions are themselves case selects out of tables precombined over
the same bits.

\paragraph{The leaf round: zero multiplications.}
At the leaf layer the entries are \emph{defined} as
$L[i] = 1 + m_{L,i}\,\tau_L[i]$ and $R[i] = 1 + m_{R,i}\,\tau_R[i]$,
with $m$ the committed bits (\code{lbits}, \code{rbits}) and $\tau$ the
shared halves.  Expanding one slot and writing
$m_{L\varepsilon} = m_{L,2b+\varepsilon}$:
\begin{align*}
  w_b\,L[2b]R[2b]
  &\;=\; w_b \;+\;
     \underbrace{m_{L0}\,w_b\tau_{L0} + m_{R0}\,w_b\tau_{R0}
       + m_{L0}m_{R0}\,w_b\tau_{L0}\tau_{R0}}_{
       \code{t\_a0}[\,(b{\ll}2)\,|\,m_{L0}\,|\,m_{R0}{\ll}1\,]} , \\[2pt]
  w_b\,\Delta L_b\,\Delta R_b
  &\;=\; w_b\big(m_{L0}\tau_{L0} + m_{L1}\tau_{L1}\big)
         \big(m_{R0}\tau_{R0} + m_{R1}\tau_{R1}\big)
   \;=\; \sum_{\varepsilon,\delta \in \bits}
         m_{L\varepsilon}m_{R\delta}\; w_b\tau_{L\varepsilon}\tau_{R\delta} ,
\end{align*}
the second line using the char-2 cancellation of the $1$s inside
$\Delta L$ and $\Delta R$ --- this is where ``bit-affine'' pays.  Both
lines are subset sums of the \emph{eight} products
$w_b\tau_{L\varepsilon}$, $w_b\tau_{R\delta}$,
$w_b\tau_{L\varepsilon}\tau_{R\delta}$ of that slot, and the leftover
constants $w_b$ sum to one per-set scalar (\code{w\_sum}) added once at
the end of the round.  \code{build\_leaf\_tables} precombines the
subsets into three per-position tables --- \code{t\_a0} and
\code{t\_a1} (4 cases each: the even and the odd entries) and the
$\Delta\Delta$ table (16 cases) --- for 8 multiplies and ${\approx}20$
additions per slot, once per $\tau$ set.  A \emph{tree's} round-1
message then costs, per slot: one aligned 2-bit field per side (32 slots
to a \code{u64}), three unconditional indexed loads, three additions.
No multiplications, and no branch on a committed bit --- a branchy
skip-the-multiply-by-one variant was measured slower, 50/50 random bits
making it mispredict-bound.

\paragraph{Two forms of the $\Delta\Delta$ table.}
The 16 $\Delta\Delta$ cases are subset sums of only four products, so
the table has an interchangeable factored form (\code{LeafA2}, selected
by \code{F2Z\_LEAF\_A2\_FACTORED}, auto at $2^{k-1} \ge 2^{15}$):
\emph{precombined} spends $16\cdot2^{k-1}$ entries for one load per
slot; \emph{factored} keeps the four raw cross products
($4\cdot2^{k-1}$ entries, one sequential 64-byte line per slot) and
selects them with four branchless masked adds that reduce pairwise, so
the accumulator still takes one add.  The trade is footprint against
work: the precombined leaf tables are $384\cdot2^{k-1}$ bytes
(12.6\,MB at $k = 16$), and once they stop co-residing in the
P-cluster L2 the factored form wins outright --- at $n = 30$ the leaf
round drops $2.1\times$ ($307 \to 147$\,ms, prove $-6.5\,\%$) and
becomes far less run-to-run volatile, while at $n = 26$ (6.3\,MB, still
resident) it loses ${\approx}1.2$\,ms.  Both accumulate the identical
field element (\code{leaf\_a2\_forms\_agree} pins it case by case).

\paragraph{Folding without materialising, and the stash.}
Round 1's fold must produce round 2's entries.  On a bit-affine side,
\[
  v' \;=\; v_0 + \rho\,(v_1 + v_0) \;=\; (1+\rho)\,v_0 + \rho\,v_1
      \;=\; 1 + m_0\,(1+\rho)\tau_0 + m_1\,\rho\,\tau_1 ,
\]
since $(1+\rho) + \rho = 1$: the folded entry is a 4-case function of
the bit pair $(m_0, m_1)$ --- Identity~\ref{id:fold} made concrete.
\code{build\_leaf\_fold\_tables} tabulates those four cases per
position, so a tree's fold is one indexed load per entry.

The deeper variants do not \emph{execute} that fold; they \emph{keep}
it.  Its table has exactly the layout of a level-$(d{-}1)$ case set
(\code{Pair2TauSet}: 4 cases per position, two sides), so
\code{Leaf2Bits} stashes it as round 2's \emph{value} tables and runs
the 16-case product-layer body over them, materialising only at round
2's fold.  \code{Leaf3Bits} iterates once more: round 2's fold tables
(16 cases per position) become round 3's value tables, read inline, and
dense buffers appear only at round 3's fold.  \code{Pair3Bits} does the
same one level up.  The economics are the point: materialising at round
$j$'s fold costs $2^{k-j}$ values per side \emph{per tree}, whereas the
stash costs $4\cdot2^{k-j}$ entries (a 4-case set) or $16\cdot2^{k-j}$
(a 16-case one) per side \emph{per $\tau$ set} --- a factor
$\mathit{live}/4$ resp.\ $\mathit{live}/16$, i.e.\ $512\times$ and
$128\times$ at the deployed $s = 11$.  It is the LUT prefix depth, not
the ladder, that fixes the bottom layers' resident set.

\paragraph{The 16-case body.}
Four rounds share one body (\code{pair2\_factored\_body}): round 1 of
\code{Pair2Bits} and of \code{Pair3Bits} over $(\code{te}, \code{to})$,
and round 2 of \code{Leaf2Bits} and of \code{Leaf3Bits} over the
stashed value sets.  Slot $b$'s two entries per side are 4-case selects, so the slot
is keyed by $(c_{E0}, c_{E1}, c_{O0}, c_{O1})$: the accumulators want
the weighted product $w\,\code{TE}[c_{E0}]\,\code{TO}[c_{O0}]$ at the
even position, its odd twin, and the product of the two differences.
Two forms compute the same field elements:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{precombined} (\code{F2Z\_PAIR2\_FACTORED=0}): four 16-case
  tables per slot ($\code{t\_a0}$, $\code{t\_a1}$, the weighted
  $\Delta\code{TE}$, the raw $\Delta\code{TO}$), so a slot is three
  loads and \emph{one} wide multiply --- but the four tables hold
  $4\cdot16\cdot2^{k-1} = 2^{k+5}$ entries, $2^{k+9}$ bytes, 16\,MiB at
  the $n = 28$ pair layer ($k = 15$), and the four gather streams' misses
  dominate the round;
\item \emph{factored} (the default): only the suffix-weighted
  $\code{wte}[i] = V_1[i \gg 3]\cdot\code{te}[i]$ is built, and the body
  recombines against the set's raw \code{to} --- three wide multiplies
  per slot against a streamed set of $2\cdot4\cdot2^k$ entries,
  $2^{k+7}$ bytes: $4\times$ smaller, and L2-resident where the other
  is not.
\end{itemize}
The two agree exactly because distributivity is exact:
$w\code{TE}_0 + w\code{TE}_1$ \emph{is} the precombined
$w(\code{TE}_0 + \code{TE}_1)$, the products keep the build's
association $(w\cdot\code{TE})\cdot\code{TO}$, and the deferred
reduction is $\Ftwo$-linear.  The lesson generalises to the inline
rounds below: multiplies are cheap next to bytes that miss.

and never a straddling one (the E/O offset $2^k$ is even at every round,
so the O-side window stays inside its word too):

The stashed tables stay keyed by the \emph{original} committed bits
throughout --- a \code{Leaf3Bits} round-3 entry is
$f[(p{\ll}4)|(c_0{\ll}2)|c_1]$ over the two adjacent original bit pairs
of positions $4p \dots 4p{+}3$, and \code{Pair3Bits}' round-2 key

is why nothing per-tree is written between rounds.  The inline rounds
(\code{Leaf3} round 3, \code{Pair3} round 2, \code{T4Bits} round 1) then
just run the ordinary dense single-pair body over four gathered values:
no precombined slot tables, because at that width the table would cost
more bytes than the multiplies it saves.

\paragraph{The 4-leaf round (\code{T4Bits}, L/8 only).}
One level further up, an entry is a single 16-case select from the
shared $T4$ --- one gather per value, read inline in round 1, dense
buffers at its fold.  The layer's input level is neither stored nor
regenerated; combined with the JIT layer below it, this is what makes
L/8 an eighth.

\paragraph{Prefetch.}
Every LUT gather is data-dependent (committed bits pick the line), which
defeats the hardware prefetcher, but the indices are cheaply recomputable
ahead from the sequential bit words.  Both the stash-gather rounds
(\code{F2Z\_LUT\_PRFM}: \code{Pair3} round 2, \code{Leaf3} round 3, and
their two materialising folds) and the $T4$ build/JIT sites
(\code{F2Z\_T4\_PRFM}) issue \code{prfm pldl1keep} 16 slots ahead,
size-gated to the DRAM-scale shapes --- they \emph{lose} at L2-resident
sizes, where the index recompute and the extra load-store pressure beat
an L2 hit.  Measured at $n = 30$: the four stash scopes drop 20--25\,\%
for $-4.8\,\%$ prove, and the $T4$ sites $-5.3\,\%$; at $n \le 28$ both
are off by default.  Semantically inert.

\subsection{Why byte-identity holds, and how it is pinned}
\label{sec:identity}

Every device above is a re-\emph{association} of the same field
arithmetic, never a different computation.  Four principles cover all of
them:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Precombination is distributivity.}  A case-table entry is a
  subset sum (or a product of subset sums) of terms the dense path would
  compute per entry; field addition and multiplication are exact and
  associative, so the accumulated sums are the same elements.
\item \emph{Char-2 cancellation is an identity.}  $1 + 1 = 0$ removes the
  leaves' $1$s from every difference, and $(1+\rho) + \rho = 1$ keeps a
  folded bit-affine entry affine; these are the rewritings that make the
  leaf round multiply-free and the fold tables 4-case.
\item \emph{Wide accumulation reduces to the same value.}  Deferring the
  polynomial reduction to one per accumulator per round is exact because
  the reduction is $\Ftwo$-linear --- and, for the same reason,
  accumulation order does not matter, which is what licenses the fused
  JIT generation passes.
\item \emph{Fusion reorders passes, not values.}  Deferred folds, the
  fused fold+message pass, the double-fold grid and the JIT
  pre-round hooks all emit the same field elements in the same
  transcript order; only the physical traffic changes.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the lazy prover does not merely ``verify the same'' --- it
emits the \emph{same transcript}, byte for byte, so the verifier and the
proof codec never learn which schedule ran.
\code{lazy\_matches\_eager} pins this against the eager prover across
depths 3--9 (covering the eager fallback, the depth-4 degenerate JIT
edge, the scalar and word-wise $T4$ generation paths at
$q_1 < 64$, $= 64$, $= 128$), $W \in \{1, 4\}$, and \emph{every}
schedule (L/2, L/4, L/8): roots, every layer's phase-A and phase-B
messages, every closing pair, the exit claim, and a challenge drawn
\emph{after} the run to compare the final transcript states.
\code{lazy\_multi\_matches\_eager} does the same for the batched forest
(\S\ref{sec:variants}) and \code{col\_elision\_matches\_full} for the
elided one.  This is what makes the knob inventory of
Table~\ref{tab:knobs} safe: every knob changes scheduling, never
semantics.

\section{Memory and cost}
\label{sec:costs}

\paragraph{Memory model.}
With 16-byte $\K$-elements and $W = 1$ (so $2^{d+s} = 2^n$ leaves), the
resident forest set is capped by the largest stage of the ladder:
\[
  \text{L/4:}\quad
  {\approx}\,2^{n-2}\cdot 16\,\mathrm{B} = 2^n\cdot 4\,\mathrm{B},
  \qquad
  \text{L/8:}\quad
  {\approx}\,2^{n-3}\cdot 16\,\mathrm{B} = 2^n\cdot 2\,\mathrm{B},
\]
(the stored chain and the JIT level are the same size and not
simultaneously live: the chain is consumed front-first,
\code{mem::take} per layer, before the JIT layer runs).  On top sit the
commit hint (${\approx}\,2^n\cdot 0.7$\,B: packed rows/columns,
codeword, Merkle tree), the transposed bit halves ($2^n/8$\,B), and the
shared tables ($O(2^d)$ per set; the largest, $T4$, is
$16\cdot 2^{d-2}$ entries --- 8\,MiB at $d = 17$).  The model matches
the measured peaks below to within ${\approx}5\,\%$; it is why the
16\,GB ceiling sits at $n = 32$ (under \code{l8}) and why $n \ge 31$
requires \code{l8} at all.

\paragraph{Measured (Apple M4 16\,GB, LTO + \code{unchecked}, fusion
defaults, rate-1/2 $k{=}4$ configs, medians; 2026-07-18).}
The \code{forest+presum} column is the phase this note describes (the
$\alpha$-power tables, the forest proper, and the pre-sumcheck of
\S\ref{sec:discharge}); its share of prove rises from 81\,\% to
${\approx}96\,\%$.

\section{The exit claim and its discharge}
\label{sec:discharge}

The forest ends holding $e_d \approx \mle{\Lambda}(z_x, z_c)$ --- still
entangled with the $\alpha$-power weights.  Two further steps
(\code{prove\_int\_eval\_merged\_common}) turn it into something the
opener understands; they are the ``presum'' half of the
\code{forest+presum} phase.

\paragraph{From the exit claim to a bit-MLE claim.}
By \eqref{eq:leaves} and multilinearity (the all-ones table extends to
$1$):
\[
  e_d - 1
  \;=\; \sum_{i, c} \eq(i, z_x)\,\eq(c, z_c)\, M[c][i]\,\tau(i)
  \;=\; \sum_{i \in \bits^d} q_{\mathrm{rowbit}}[i]\cdot m[i],
\]
with $q_{\mathrm{rowbit}}[i] = \eq(i, z_x)\,\tau(i)$
(\code{row\_bit\_weights} at $\rho = z_x$; its $2^t$
${\approx}q_{\mathrm{bits}}$-bit exponentiations go through the same
fixed-base comb) and $m[i] = \sum_c \eq(c, z_c)\,M[c][i]$ the
$z_c$-combined row (\code{xi\_combined\_rows}).  A $d$-round
\emph{degree-2} sumcheck over this product
(\code{MultiDegreeSumcheck}, one group) binds $i \to r^*$; dividing the
final expected evaluation by the public
$\smash{\mle{q_{\mathrm{rowbit}}}}(r^*)$ leaves
$\mu = \mle{M}(r^*, z_c)$ --- a pure bit-MLE evaluation at
$(r^* \| z_c)$, every $\alpha$-dependent factor stripped to the
verifier's side.  From here the ring switch sends the 128 partial
evaluations $s_v$, the $L$ chunks' residuals are $\eta$-combined, and
one recursive Ligerito call certifies the batch against the commitment
--- verifier note \S6, out of scope here.

\paragraph{The chunked flow.}
\code{prove\_mle\_eval\_mod\_q\_ligerito} runs the full
forest~$+$~presum \emph{per chunk}, sequentially on one transcript
(chunk $l$'s $\alpha$-power tables are built and dropped inside its
run), then does all ring-switch messages, then the single Ligerito
call.  Per chunk the proof stream carries: the \code{MergedForestProof},
the folds $u^{(l)}$, the pre-sumcheck, and $s_v$
(\code{proof\_codec.rs}).

\section{Variants: the batched forest and the RLC families}
\label{sec:variants}

\paragraph{Multi-claim batching (\code{prove\_merged\_forest\_lazy\_multi}).}
$N$ same-shape claims --- each $2^s$ trees over its own bits and
$\tau$ chains --- run as \emph{one} forest of $N\cdot 2^s$ trees, tree
index $(\nu \ll s)\,|\,c$ with the claim index high, sharing every
layer's rounds and messages.  Per-claim table selection rides the
driver's \code{tau\_set} index (the kernels are untouched); table sets
are deduplicated by $\tau$-chain identity, so same-point claims share
one set.  $N$ must be a power of two; callers pad with zero-weight
dummies, whose $\tau$ chains are all $1$ and whose leaves are therefore
identically $1$ regardless of the bits they carry.  L/4 and L/8 are both
honoured (L/2 is a single-prover schedule; the batched path reads the
knob as L/4-or-L/8); pinned byte-identical to the eager prover over the
concatenated leaf tables, dummy arm included.

\paragraph{RLC claim families (\textsc{experimental}).}
The mod-$q$ RLC families (\code{docs/rlc-family-note/}) replace the
bit-affine leaf with a $2^j$-case select on $j$ committed column bits:
$\Lambda[c][i] = \code{case\_pow}[i][m(i,c)]$, case $0 = \alpha^0 = 1$.
For $j = 2$ (\code{prove\_merged\_forest\_lazy\_rlc2}) this is a pure
\emph{rewiring} of the machinery above, one level shifted: the leaf
layer is structurally a product layer (\code{Pair3Bits} over
$\code{te}[4y{+}m] = \code{case\_pow}[y][m]$), level $d{-}1$ is a
\code{T4Bits} round over $\code{te}\cdot\code{to}$ products, level
$d{-}2$ is Dense JIT from paired gathers, and the chain tops at $d{-}3$
--- the L/4 shape with untouched kernels, hence byte-identical to the
eager forest over the same leaves (pinned).  For $j = 3, 4$
(\code{prove\_merged\_forest\_lazy\_rlc\_general}) no $2^j$-case leaf
kernels exist yet, so the bottom layers run Dense with JIT-generated
buffers gathered from a shared $2^{2j}$-case top-pair product table
$T2$; dedicated 8/16-case leaf-round kernels are the stated open lever.
(A Dense-JIT arm for the \emph{bit-affine} $j \ge 3$ forests was
measured slower than the eager path and closed.)

\section{What to trust, and what this note is not}
\label{sec:trust}

The prover described here is exercised by: the both-schedule
byte-identity pins (\code{lazy\_matches\_eager},
\code{lazy\_multi\_matches\_eager} --- roots, every message, exit
claim, transcript state, across the depth-regime and $W$ matrix), the
GKR roundtrip and tamper rejections (\code{merged\_forest\_roundtrips}),
the mod-$q$ end-to-end roundtrips with range/generator/tamper arms, and
the NEON/scalar field-pipeline equivalence
(\code{neon\_mul\_matches\_scalar\_pipeline}).  Soundness of what the
forest \emph{binds} --- the chain from the absorbed roots through the
layer sumchecks to the exit claim, the exponent injectivity, and the
oracle handoff --- is argued in the verifier note and
\code{docs/DESIGN.md}; this note's claim is narrower and mechanical:
the lazy, merged, fused prover computes \emph{the same protocol} as the
eager reference, at $O(\ell + s)$ payload per layer,
${\approx}\,2^n\cdot4$\,B (or $2^n\cdot2$\,B) of working set, and
81--96\,\% of an F2Z prove.  The standard caveats carry over unchanged:
flock's Merkle domain separation is a flagged upstream item, the
ad-hoc Ligerito config regime ($m < 22$) is unaudited, and the scheme
is not zero-knowledge.
