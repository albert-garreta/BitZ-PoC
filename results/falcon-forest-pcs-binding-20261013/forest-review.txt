Wide buckets:
- Keep exact256-bit carryless products in3*256*32=24576 bytes, compared with
 12288 bytes for reduced buckets. This avoids retaining the three-component
 Karatsuba representation, which would require36864 bytes.
- The private BucketStorage enum is wrapped by SumBuckets with a private
 field, preserving the constructor's768-entry invariant before unsafe code.
- Low/high polynomial halves are assembled exactly from the existing
 Karatsuba components. XOR accumulation cannot overflow or require a
 count-dependent reduction bound. Final reduction precedes multiplication
 by the E tables; the final dot-product reductions remain in both paths.
- At2048 columns, intermediate product/bucket reductions change4096 to768
 per row. This is not the total reduction count or a predicted speedup:
 it excludes finish-dot reductions and other field operations. Scatter
 read-modify-write width doubles and reduction traffic may hurt caches.
- Four potentially colliding lanes finish their low AND high updates in
 lane order. No gather/scatter of stale shared entries is used.
- The finish loop loads exactly four32-byte products into two512-bit
 registers, rearranges their low/high halves, reduces four lanes, and
 accumulates the original contraction. All positions are in bounds.
- Tests added for exact polynomial products versus portable arithmetic,
 XOR accumulation/reduction, scalar and reduced-bucket agreement under
 collisions/partial live groups/reuse, and canaries around wide scatter.

Retained level4:
- Existing upper construction computes16-leaf products into local tiles.
 The experiment writes each initialized tile to a second buffer, preserving
 the baseline computation of every upper level and its transcript order.
- At t14/s11 this stores2097152 field elements:32MiB additional peak capacity.
 It is not a persistent Prepared cache. Retained storage replaces the upper
 arena only after all upper levels have been proved, then serves ordinary
 level4 and subsequent JIT folding.
- Parallel ownership is identical to the existing upper builder: task y0
 owns rows y0+b*sub, and column groups partition each row. Retained and upper
 buffers are separate allocations. The pointer wrapper sees no reallocation.
- Retained length stays zero until all tasks join and every slot is written.
 Panic paths therefore never expose an uninitialized Gf slice. Copying
 MaybeUninit tiles occurs only after the product kernel initialized them.
- Retention is skipped for tiny materialized fallback and depths that do
 not visit level4. Tests cover those cases, partial columns, nibble/gather
 modes, Boolean challenges, exact complete transcript equality, and an
 independent direct16-leaf product oracle for every retained coordinate.
- Previous comments document retention's larger-geometry memory tradeoff;
 this is a new measurement for the current Falcon workload, not a claim
 that level4 retention is a previously unexplored idea.
