Reviewed factored lookup tables, twelve-worker virtual row ownership, their

Conclusion
No actionable correctness or safety findings in the reviewed changes.

Invariants checked
- Factored tables are selected only for ell=0,1,2 at k=3-ell. Each product
  depends on 1, 2 or 4 adjacent bits and stays within one nibble. The sum of
  the two sixteen-entry tables preserves constant terms and all 256 patterns.
- scale_fold multiplies both stored nibble halves by the original position's
  fold-side weight. This preserves both ordinary and pre-scaled fold results.
- Bucket-round expansion is row-local initialized scratch; all references
  remain valid through the unchanged bucket kernel. Native fold kernels read
  compact tables directly; other backends expand local scratch.
- Native x86 lookup checks at least 32 field entries before pointer loads.
  Byte-derived low/high nibble indices remain in 0..16 and 16..32. The existing
  n<=64, output-length and equality-weight checks bound permuted pattern
  positions and complete four-lane stores; remaining outputs use scalar tails.
- Factored table construction writes every slot of each disjoint 32-entry
  MaybeUninit chunk before setting Vec length. Panic before completion leaves
  length zero. Gf owns no drop resources. Arena write coverage remains intact.
- Virtual shard owner((row%64)%12) enumerates each selected row exactly once.
  Mutable row jobs are transferred as disjoint safe slices. The upper-builder
  pointer invariant remains y=y0+b*sub with distinct y0 values; its dependencies
  are written by the same task before being read.
- Shards require twelve workers, row counts divisible by 64 and at least 4 MiB.
  Smaller or unsupported shapes fall back, and the previous sixteen-worker
  cyclic path remains available. Test selector overrides restore after panic.
- Experimental selectors default to expanded tables and legacy row ownership;
  production behavior remains gated independently of explicit test selection.

Test coverage inspected
All 256 patterns against independent folded leaf products; zero, one and random
challenges; pre-scaling; scalar-oracle kernel comparisons with output guards,
SIMD/scalar tails and both weighing modes; complete forest proof equality;
eight/twelve-worker shard transitions, exhaustive row ownership and fallback
boundaries. Tests were inspected, not executed by this reviewer.

Performance limits
No speedup is established by this review. Virtual 64-shard assignment gives
six shards per block to four workers and five to eight workers, a 20% workload
imbalance to weigh against locality in the benchmark campaign. Compact-table
expansion adds row-local work and requires separate performance qualification.

Execution limits
