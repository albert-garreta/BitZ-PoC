\subsubsection{Isolating the inner-product mechanism: comparison with Binius64's machinery}\label{s:exp_binius64_ip}

We isolate the mechanism at the heart of \cref{c:core_iop} --- certifying $g^{\langle \vv, \bff\rangle} = g^{\mu}$ by grand products over $\comfield$ --- and measure it against Binius64's exponentiation machinery \cite{binius64} in the two claim shapes of \cref{s:?}: \albert{cref the comparison paragraph in the TO}
(1) $\vv$ public with a tensor decomposition and $\bff$ a committed bit vector (the shape arising in \ftwoz), and (2) both $\vv$ and $\bff$ committed $64$-bit word vectors with no tensor structure (the shape arising in Binius64's integer multiplication). All arms run multi-threaded on the same machine (MacBook Air M4, 16GB, 10 threads) over the same $\comfield=\FF_{2^{128}}$ in GHASH representation; Binius64 is upstream commit \texttt{7ad579a1} built with its parallel feature; every driver checks the resulting product root against a directly computed $g^{\mu}$; times are medians of $3$ runs. Both sides are measured \emph{before} the commitment opening: the \ftwoz\ column is the grand-product (GKR forest) phase of the prover, with the full prover including the commitment opening in parentheses.

\paragraph{Shape (1).} We realize the claim with Binius64's own components --- their eager product-tree prover over the bit-affine leaves $1 + \bff_i\cdot (g^{\vv_i}-1)$, with the leaf claim discharged by their selector mlecheck --- in two arms: \emph{structure-oblivious} (one product of length $\ell$; the leaf values cost $\ell$ exponentiations), and \emph{split-gifted}, where we hand their prover the tensor split for free ($\ell_1$ exponentiations and $\ell_2$ batched subcube products), which their machinery cannot exploit further: the shared base vector must still be materialized at full length $\ell$ and folded densely each round, whereas the \ftwoz\ prover replaces it by the lookup tables of \cref{s:gkr_low_entropy}. Weights are $100$-bit, matching the single-chunk regime of \cref{s:instantiation}.

The oblivious arm is dominated ($>90\%$) by the $\ell$ leaf exponentiations, confirming the $O(\ell_1)$-vs-$O(\ell)$ separation of \cref{s:gkr_low_entropy} (the verifier-side separation is analogous); it also cannot express the production claim at all once the tensor factors exceed the exponent budget, since monolithic weights $\vv^{(2)}_j\cdot \vv^{(1)}_i$ of two $\approx 100$-bit factors overflow $\mathsf{ord}(g)$. The residual $1.7\times$--$18\times$ of the split-gifted arm, at $15$--$19\times$ the memory, is the price of eager layer materialization and dense base folding against the lookup tables of \cref{s:gkr_low_entropy}, and it grows with $\ell$ as the eager working set leaves cache.

\paragraph{Shape (2).} Conversely, shape (2) is bilinear in the witness, and \ftwoz\ has no mechanism for it: there is no public factor to tabulate. Binius64's native pipeline (per-row variable-base exponentiation $g^{\vv_i \bff_i}$ through its four product trees, Frobenius selector discharge and committed LogUp* power-table lookup, plus one accumulation product over the $2^i$ row roots, which we add) proves $\langle \vv,\bff\rangle = \mu \bmod 2^{128}-1$ in the times below; it is strong at cache-resident sizes ($0.79\,\mu$s per $64\times 64$ product at $2^{16}$ rows) and degrades beyond them, hitting a memory wall at $2^{21}$--$2^{22}$ rows ($\approx 5.5$ KiB of eagerly materialized tree layers per row).

Since a shape-(2) instance at $2^i$ rows commits $2^{i+7}$ witness bits, the two shapes are best compared at equal committed input: shape (2) at $2^{16}/2^{18}/2^{20}$ rows against shape (1) at $\ell = 2^{23}/2^{25}/2^{27}$, where \ftwoz\ measures $19.4/58.1/188.3$ ms. Per committed witness bit the ratio is $2.7\times$, $4.0\times$ and $11.2\times$ in favor of \ftwoz\ --- widening with size for the same cache reasons --- at $6$--$8\times$ less memory. The residual factor is the cost of certifying a claim that is bilinear in the witness rather than linear with public structured weights: each mechanism is efficient at the claim shape it was built for, and neither side's optimizations transfer across that boundary.
