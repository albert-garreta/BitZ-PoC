\subsubsection{Raw performance of \ftwoz}

We benchmark the \ftwoz polynomial commitment scheme on binary committed
witnesses containing $2^i$ bits, for $20\leq i\leq 30$.  All measurements use
one-bit words, eight Rayon threads, and the \texttt{custom:3:4} Ligerito
configuration (rate $1/8$ and $k=4$).  For $i\in\{20,21\}$, the same rate and
$k$ are retained while the size-dependent Johnson and out-of-domain parameters
are mechanically re-derived from the $i=22$ policy template.

\Cref{tab:f2z-pcs-headline} reports medians over 21 clean measured repetitions
per exponent, following one excluded warm-up.  The prover measurement covers
the PCS opening/proving work and excludes the initial commitment.  The proof
size is the exact serialized size.  Peak memory is measured separately: it is
one prover run's high-water mark of outstanding Rust heap bytes, including the
live commitment hint, rather than a percentile over the 21 timing repetitions.

To expose prover cost, we separately instrument the prover span.  Its eight
direct child spans are disjoint and ordered: construction of the powers-of-two
claims (Claims), the merged forest proof (Forest), the folded evaluation vector
(Fold-$v$), pre-sumcheck table construction (Pre-SC tbl.), the pre-sumcheck
(Pre-SC), ring switching (Ring sw.), basis combination (Combine), and the
recursive Ligerito opening (Ligerito).  These spans cover between $96.7\%$ and
$100.0\%$ of the prover span; the small remaining gaps are untagged orchestration
costs.  For each exponent, \cref{tab:f2z-pcs-prover-breakdown} uses 21 fresh
instrumented trials following one excluded warm-up and reports the per-phase
$P_{50}$ time.  These are marginal phase medians, so values from different
columns need not add to the median total.  Instrumentation makes this capture
